Atlas / Work

No project sits alone.

My work moves across national networks, higher education, science interpretation, evaluation, and place-based partnership. The useful unit is not the sector. It is the relationship between people, institutions, resources, evidence, and what becomes possible next.

How to read the atlas

Each project is a coordinate inside the same practice. Use the lenses to change emphasis, then look for what travels between projects: a participation model becomes a launch strategy; field learning changes partnership practice; evaluation reshapes the next product.

Six projects. Five recurring lenses. One evolving system of practice.

The lenses are intentionally overlapping. A network project can also be evidence work. A science product can also be institutional learning. The point is to show the joins.

Transfers / 02

The useful part is what travels.

Projects matter on their own. Practice becomes visible when a lesson from one context changes how the next problem is framed.

Webb Community Events→Roman field readiness

Partner-centered participation, modular support, and early host preparation become a more deliberate launch-era system.

STORIMap pilot→Future interpretation

Audience strategy and evaluation turn one digital experiment into a framework for better kickoff, structure, accessibility, and iteration.

Hawaiʻi field collaboration→Partnership practice

Place, authority, listening, and authorship become operational questions rather than values left outside the project plan.

Higher-ed research→Institutional design

Following where learning loses force sharpens a broader practice: locate the rule, identify who holds authority, and reduce avoidable navigation.
