The invitation

The project brief was a video. The real work was building the conditions for a good partnership.

Our visit strengthened an existing relationship between the Space Telescope Science Institute, NASA’s Universe of Learning, and ʻImiloa. The purpose was not simply to collect material for a science story. It was to deepen our understanding of our partners’ work and consider what we could responsibly co-create.

At ʻImiloa, conversations with Kaʻiu Kimura and the team, time in the exhibits, and the planetarium’s One Sky Project opened a wider view of the partnership. Astronomy was present alongside language, genealogy, land, and community. Those connections mattered to how we approached the next conversation, not only to what appeared on screen.

One distinction became especially important in my reflection: Hawaiian Knowledge Systems, rather than “culture” as an added layer of interpretation. The knowledge did not originate with our visiting team. Our responsibility was to listen, ask careful questions, and let what we learned shape the collaboration.

The week’s shared learning

Connection before production.

  1. Pilina · Connection

    Introductions and time at ʻImiloa established relationships and helped us understand the center’s approach to learning.

  2. Kuleana · Responsibility

    At Puʻu Huluhulu and Maunakea, conversations turned to the responsibilities of observatories and scientists, and the significance of place.

  3. Moʻokūʻauhau a me Aloha ʻĀina

    Time at Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park brought genealogy and love of the land into our learning. The landscape was not a backdrop.

  4. Hoʻokipa · Hospitality

    The final days brought us back to the table: sharing the “Physics of Pō” video work, receiving feedback, and discussing a path forward together.

My contribution

Helping the team carry the learning forward.

My leadership involved more than getting six people to the same place. It meant helping turn a week of conversations and shared experiences into something our institutions could act on without losing what made the exchange meaningful.

Lead the visiting team

I led our six-person team through the field collaboration, working within an experience shaped by our hosts’ knowledge of the place and the partnership.

Make the learning usable

I documented the visit’s themes and practical implications in a detailed reflection for leadership. That synthesis connected what we had experienced with decisions about language, collaborative media, youth engagement, and continuing relationships.

Frame a path forward

The September 17 wrap-up brought priorities, next steps, partner roles, and continuity into the same discussion. Our proposed directions included collaborative content, youth mentorship, and regular opportunities for feedback. These were directions for future work, not a list of programs already delivered.

Keep authorship and authority clear

I led the team and helped translate the exchange into partnership planning. The cultural knowledge, scientific contributions, and media production were collaborative. Leading the project did not make me the author of the knowledge our partners shared.

The culminating video

Stars in the Sea

The video places passages from the Kumulipo in conversation with an astronomical account of the early universe, stars, and galaxies. Language, imagery, and pacing carry the story together.

The film is a shared outcome. The partnership behind it is the larger story: the conversations and responsibilities that shaped what we could make together.

What stayed with me

A partnership has to change how you work.

I am proud of the video. I am also proud of the less visible work: making room for conversation, paying attention to language, and taking responsibility for what happens after a visit ends.

This experience sharpened the way I think about institutional partnerships. A finished resource matters, but so do the questions behind it: whose knowledge is shaping the work, who has room to respond, what decisions change because of the collaboration, and what commitments remain after the project is complete?
