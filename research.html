Inquiry / 00 · Harvard 2024–2026

When does learning actually count?

I study the point where learning meets institutional authority: who can recognize it, what that recognition obligates an institution to do, and whether a learner can rely on the promise after the learning has already happened.

The questions are analytical, but they are also personal. I began college at Northampton Community College in 2009 and transferred to the University of Pittsburgh in 2011.

Years later, I returned to transfer, recognition, and institutional responsibility with a different vocabulary and the same underlying question: what allows a learner’s progress to keep moving across systems?

Thesis / 01

Recognition makes learning visible. Application makes learning move.

When Does Learning Count? Recognition, Authority, and Binding Pathways in U.S. Higher Education examined apprenticeship-to-degree pathways as a governance problem. A learner can complete the work, demonstrate mastery, earn a credential, and still discover that the learning does not satisfy anything consequential.

My independent study argues that the missing mechanism is binding recognition: a pre-committed institutional obligation to honor validated learning under defined conditions. Judgment does not disappear; it moves upstream into the design of standards, evidence, approval, and audit.

Scale cannot depend on heroics. Scale needs rules.

Cases / 03

Two governance models reveal different parts of the problem: internal application and external portability.

Depth without portability. Portability without obligation.

Reach University

Embedded recognition

Reach redesigns the degree around workplace learning. Assessment and application are built into the model, so work counts inside the institution.

What it proves

Institutions can move judgment upstream and redistribute assessment authority.

What remains

Recognition that binds internally does not automatically travel to another institution.

RACC

Networked recognition

The Registered Apprenticeship–College Consortium helps apprenticeship learning travel through shared translation and credit-recognition structures.

What it proves

Systems can widen the legibility and portability of validated learning.

What remains

Recognized credit may still become elective credit, or return to case-by-case discretion.

Tools / 04

The research moved from argument to usable intervention.

The work began with a governance question: who gets to say what counts as college? It ended with an Applied-Credit Reliability Diagnostic that follows credit until it becomes usable progress, or loses force.

Integrative view

Storytelling + systems.

My personal career map became a synthesis of the degree: early wonder, community college and transfer, professional formation, work paths, and meaning converging in a practice built around education strategy and storytelling.

The throughline is not a sector. It is a way of seeing: follow the learner, locate the rule, study who holds authority, and design conditions in which people can move with dignity.

Practice / 05

The value of research is not that it stays separate from practice. It is that it changes what I notice and what I design.

Five commitments sharpened at Harvard.

Move judgment upstream.

Rigor is strengthened when standards, evidence, authority, and application are decided clearly before an individual must petition.

Design beyond navigation.

Equity cannot mean teaching people to survive broken processes more skillfully. Systems should reduce the need for heroic navigation.

Treat belonging as infrastructure.

Belonging frees cognitive bandwidth; credible signals of mattering stabilize identity and make authorship possible.

Ask what rule the number creates.

Metrics and agreements allocate risk. A system becomes legible when we identify who is protected and who becomes the flexible variable.

Let reflection change the design.

Reflection is not retreat from action. It is disciplined interpretation that protects complexity from being flattened by urgency.
