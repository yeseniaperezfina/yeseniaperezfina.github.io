About · The throughline

I build academic programs strong enough to expand a young person’s sense of what is possible.

My work joins educational vision with the people, operations, resources, and evidence required to make that promise real across classrooms, camps, museums, digital spaces, and national learning networks.

The work beneath the work

I know what a rigorous enrichment program can change because one changed me.

Beginning in fourth grade, Lehigh University’s STAR program gave me Saturdays and summers filled with tutors, professors, speakers, field experiences, camps, and a community that treated intellectual curiosity as something worth cultivating.

That experience did more than enrich my education. It widened the map. It taught me that rigor could be an expression of belief in a young person and that belonging in academic spaces could be learned through sustained invitation, challenge, and care.

My own route later moved through community college, a state university, private higher education, and Harvard. That path taught me that advanced potential does not arrive through one tidy pipeline. Institutions must learn to recognize it, develop it, and build routes through which it can become meaningful opportunity.

My leadership practice

I work across the full architecture of a program.

01

Academic direction

Clarify learning outcomes, program identity, audience needs, and the experience a portfolio should create across formats.

02

People and culture

Supervise staff, train educators and large docent communities, develop talent, and create clear expectations that support strong delivery.

03

Operations and stewardship

Lead budgets, grants, funding cycles, timelines, partners, implementation systems, and the practical decisions that keep quality intact.

04

Evidence and renewal

Use evaluation, field intelligence, and reflection to improve programs, make strategic choices, and strengthen institutional learning.

Harvard · 2026

Leadership is the work of turning possibility into a structure people can trust.

I completed my Ed.M. in Educational Leadership at the Harvard Graduate School of Education while continuing my work at STScI. My research explored recognition governance, postsecondary value, institutional trust, workforce pathways, and the conditions that determine whether learning leads anywhere consequential.

At Convocation, I was selected as one of three student speakers. “What Held” asked what becomes possible when attention is treated as care, rigor is not confused with cruelty, and institutions make room for people with full and complicated lives.

Values in practice

Not decoration. Operating principles.

Rigor as belief

High expectations matter most when learners receive the time, support, feedback, and community needed to meet them.

Clarity as care

Educators and learners should not have to decode a system before they can succeed inside it.

Belonging with consequence

Belonging is not atmosphere alone. It changes what people can access, attempt, contribute, and carry forward.

Stewardship over spectacle

Strong programs depend on budgets, policies, staffing, training, evaluation, and maintenance that are often invisible but never optional.

A note on wonder

The moment a learner feels recognized is not the end of the work. It is the beginning of responsibility.

Sometimes that moment happens in a camp, a museum gallery, a virtual classroom, beneath a telescope, or during one Saturday program that quietly redraws the future. My work is to build institutions capable of honoring what that moment asks of them.

What I am building toward

Academic programs where young people encounter challenge, community, and a credible invitation into larger possibility.

I am drawn to senior leadership roles that unite academic strategy, program operations, people development, access, innovation, and organizational learning, especially where institutions are ready to make ambitious educational promises and build the systems required to keep them.
