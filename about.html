About me

I have spent my career helping people find their way into ideas, institutions, and futures that once felt far away.

The longer story

I am Puerto Rican, first-generation, and from Bethlehem, Pennsylvania. I came to education first as a child whose own map of possibility was widened by other people.

Beginning in fourth grade, Lehigh University’s STAR program gave me Saturdays and summers filled with tutors, professors, field experiences, camps, and adults who treated curiosity as something worth cultivating. The program did not hand me a neat path. It gave me a larger sense of what might be possible and a community that expected me to keep reaching toward it.

My route through higher education was not linear. It moved through community college, a state university, Georgetown, and eventually Harvard. That path made me attentive to the ways institutions recognize potential, the ways they miss it, and the amount of invisible navigation many people are expected to perform before they can even begin the work they came to do.

I started my career in museums, where learning was physical, social, and alive. I taught children, worked with families, led camps, trained educators and docents, managed programs, and learned how much the quality of a public experience depends on what happens long before anyone walks into the room.

At the Space Telescope Science Institute, my canvas became larger. I now work across NASA missions, national partner networks, digital interpretation, community engagement, evaluation, and public storytelling. Much of my role is connective: helping scientists, educators, designers, writers, evaluators, and institutions build something coherent together.

At Harvard, I studied educational leadership while continuing that work. I became more interested in the institutional layer beneath every learning experience: who makes decisions, what gets recognized, how resources move, and whether an organization’s systems can keep the promises its language makes.

What I care about

Clarity without simplification. Rigor without cruelty. Belonging with consequence.

I believe people should not have to decode a system before they can succeed inside it.

I believe educators and community partners are not delivery mechanisms. They are knowledgeable interpreters and co-designers.

I believe attention is a form of care, and that maintenance, follow-through, budgets, training, and evaluation are part of the imaginative work.

I believe wonder matters, especially when it changes what someone thinks they are allowed to ask, understand, or become.

My speech was about the practices that make room for people with full lives: attention, generosity, rigor, and the willingness to carry one another without pretending the carrying is effortless.

What I am looking toward

Work that is intellectually alive, operationally honest, and useful to real people.

I am drawn to roles where I can help a team make sense of complexity, build the structures around a strong idea, and create learning experiences or products that people can use with confidence. I want to keep working at the seam between education, product, strategy, and public purpose without sanding away the human texture that makes the work matter.
