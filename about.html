About me

I care about what institutions make possible, and what people have to navigate to reach it.

The work, and the route into it

I’m a program and portfolio strategist, educator, and writer. For more than eleven years, I have worked across science, education, arts and culture, and higher education, turning ambitious public-facing ideas into programs, partnerships, and systems people can actually use. My interest in that work is personal.

I am Puerto Rican, first-generation, and from Bethlehem, Pennsylvania. Beginning in fourth grade, Lehigh University’s STAR program widened my own map through sustained learning, community, and adults who treated curiosity as something worth cultivating.

My route through higher education was not linear. It moved through community college, a state university, Georgetown, and eventually Harvard. That path made me attentive to the ways institutions recognize potential, the ways they miss it, and the amount of invisible navigation many people are expected to perform before they can even begin the work they came to do.

I began my career in museums, where I learned that the quality of a public experience depends on what happens long before anyone enters the room. Preparation, trust, budgets, training, and follow-through are not separate from the learning. They are part of the operating system that makes it possible.

That lesson still guides my work at the Space Telescope Science Institute. Across NASA missions, I connect scientists, educators, designers, evaluators, and community partners. I lead program planning, funding cycles, partner strategy, implementation support, and evaluation for the Informal Learning Network, alongside work on digital interpretation and national community engagement. The scale has changed; the attention to what people and institutions need in order to carry the work has not.

One collaboration I am especially proud of took our team to Hawaiʻi to learn and work with ʻImiloa Astronomy Center. I led a six-person visiting team, but our hosts shaped how we understood the place and the knowledge we were there to encounter. The partnership and the video that grew from it remind me that listening is not preparation for the real work. It is part of it.

At Harvard, I took those practical questions into my study of higher education: who makes decisions, what gets recognized, how resources move, and whether an institution can keep the promises it makes to learners. I am interested in how those choices shape someone’s actual possibilities, not only their experience of a single program.

Writing gives me another way to stay with a question. In The Echo Jar, I write more personally, with room for uncertainty, wonder, and things I have not yet found a tidy way to explain.

What I care about

Clarity without simplification. Rigor without cruelty. Belonging with consequence.

I believe people should not have to decode a system before they can succeed inside it.

I believe educators and community partners are not delivery mechanisms. They are knowledgeable interpreters and co-designers.

I believe attention is a form of care, and that maintenance, follow-through, budgets, training, and evaluation are part of the imaginative work.

I believe wonder matters, especially when it changes what someone thinks they are allowed to ask, understand, or become.

What I carry forward

Good intentions need thoughtful decisions and sustained care.

I want to help institutions make good on their commitments to learning and public life. Whether the setting is philanthropy, higher education, or a science organization, I bring experience setting program direction, working through funding decisions, supporting partnerships, aligning cross-functional teams, and using evidence to improve the work. I also bring the perspective of someone who knows how much it matters when an institution makes room.
