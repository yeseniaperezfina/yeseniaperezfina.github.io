01

The leadership challenge

A national portfolio must create consistency without erasing local intelligence.

NASA can make extraordinary knowledge available. That alone does not make it locally meaningful, culturally responsive, operationally feasible, or usable by the educators who meet audiences every day.

The work requires more than content distribution. It requires a disciplined annual cycle: priorities, calls for participation, proposal and budget review, funding decisions, implementation support, accountability, evaluation, and renewal. It also requires enough flexibility for trusted local institutions to design for their own communities.

My contribution

I connect academic strategy with the machinery required to deliver it.

My role sits between institutional ambition and partner reality. I help make sure the portfolio’s learning goals, resources, funding, expectations, evidence, and relationships operate as one system.

A

Set portfolio direction

Translate NASA mission priorities, audience needs, and field trends into coherent annual opportunities and program expectations.

B

Steward grants and funding cycles

Manage calls, proposal and budget review, awards, implementation requirements, spending visibility, deliverables, and reporting across the network.

C

Support program delivery

Guide institutional partners through planning, adaptation, professional learning, problem-solving, and the practical work of moving from proposal to learner experience.

D

Turn evidence into decisions

Use evaluation, partner feedback, audience research, and implementation lessons to improve supports and inform future portfolio choices.

Field note · Hawai‘i

Place is not the backdrop to learning. It is part of the knowledge and part of the operating context.

Strong program leadership requires understanding where learning lives: in institutions, histories, staffing realities, landscapes, budgets, and relationships. Field experience keeps strategy accountable to that complexity.

03

What the network enables

One shared academic purpose. Many credible local pathways.

The network’s strength is not sameness. It is the capacity to hold common standards and goals while partners design experiences for distinct learners, communities, and institutional contexts.

Distributed educator capacity

Partners gain knowledge, relationships, resources, and confidence that extend beyond a single event or funding year.

Locally responsive delivery

Organizations can choose formats, stories, staffing models, and entry points that make sense for their audiences.

Portfolio learning

Implementation experience returns to the larger system, creating a feedback loop rather than a one-way pipeline.

Durable participation

NASA science becomes part of ongoing community learning rather than only a temporary moment of attention.

Scale, with stewardship at the center

142K+ learners.
Nearly 100 organizations.
One learning ecosystem.

The numbers describe reach. The deeper result is a network behaving less like a collection of isolated projects and more like an academic and operational system: shared goals, funded implementation, educator development, evidence, accountability, and room for local invention.

Reflection

Scale is not the multiplication of activities. It is the design of conditions under which quality can travel.

This work has reinforced a principle I carry across every program portfolio: the learner experience begins long before a learner arrives. It is shaped through budgets, staffing, training, policies, relationships, feedback systems, and the choices leaders make about what deserves to grow.
