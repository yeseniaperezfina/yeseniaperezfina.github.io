What the partnership is

Local knowledge is a starting point, not a delivery channel.

A resource can be scientifically excellent and still need a thoughtful educator to make it work in a particular room.

Museums, science centers, libraries, and other informal learning institutions bring knowledge of their audiences. NASA’s Universe of Learning brings astrophysics resources and connections to mission experts. ILN supports partners as they adapt those resources and create programming suited to local learners.

My role is to support that exchange in practical ways: clarify what is possible, make expectations understandable, help partners work through constraints, and carry what we learn into the next round of decisions. Relationships matter here because they make it possible to ask for help, share an unfinished idea, or tell us when something is not working.

My contribution

The care is in the follow-through.

Leading the network means holding program direction and day-to-day partnership work together. A promising idea needs resources, understandable requirements, time to develop, and someone who will stay in the conversation.

Steward the funding cycle

I work across calls for participation, proposal and budget review, funding processes, implementation support, and reporting. I help connect the intent of an opportunity with the practical realities of carrying it out.

Support educators through the work

I help partners plan, adapt resources, troubleshoot, and connect with useful expertise. I bring an educator’s perspective to those conversations: what will make this understandable and workable for the people using it?

Make room for learning across sites

I support professional learning and sustained relationships, so that the value of participation is not limited to a single product or grant cycle. Partners’ questions and experiences help shape what support is useful next.

Use evidence with context

I bring evaluation and partner feedback into planning. Reach tells one part of the story; I also want to know how an experience worked, what partners needed, and what they would change. Those questions help us improve the support around the program, not only count the activity.

In practice

Different audiences call for different approaches.

The public ILN collection includes more than 50 program models, from science festivals to after-school learning and early-learner activities. Monthly Cosmic Conversations offer another place for exchange.

Funding opportunities have specific eligibility requirements; the public project page currently identifies ASTC member organizations and Smithsonian Affiliations members. Participation and funding should not be described as the same thing.

FY25 · Network-wide reach

142K+ learners

Reached through ILN-supported programs, exhibitions, and outreach. This is collective reach across partners, not a count of people I taught directly.

The number matters because it represents local work. It does not, on its own, tell us what someone learned or whether a partnership will last. That is why evaluation, feedback, and continued relationships belong alongside it.

What this work asks of me

Listen closely. Make the support useful. Stay with it.

The network has taught me that strong program leadership is relational and operational at the same time. A clear budget, a useful introduction, an honest conversation, and a well-designed learning opportunity can all help a partner do something they could not have done alone.

That is the kind of partnership I want this work to make visible: a community with knowledge to contribute, not a collection of sites waiting for instructions.
